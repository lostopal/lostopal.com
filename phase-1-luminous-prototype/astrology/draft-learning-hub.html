The first shelves

What each doorway will hold.

For now, this is the honest foundation: a clear map of what belongs here and the order in which I intend to learn and build it.

☉

First collection

Planets

The moving powers. I will begin with the seven classical planets: their functions, dignities, metals, weekdays, relationships, and the different ways they act through a chart.

  • Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn
  • Traditional functions and clearly labeled interpretation
  • Planetary relationships, cycles, and useful correspondences
♊

Second collection

Signs

The twelve modes through which planetary forces express themselves, studied through element, modality, polarity, rulership, season, and the character of each sign.

  • A clear twelve-sign overview before twelve separate entries
  • No reducing a person to a single placement
  • The chosen zodiac framework named plainly
Ⅶ

Third collection

Houses

The fields of lived experience—where a planet’s action becomes concrete in life. The house system in use will be named rather than quietly treated as the only possible system.

  • The twelve fields and their core topics
  • Angular, succedent, and cadent houses
  • Traditional meanings and modern additions kept distinct
△

Fourth collection

Aspects

The geometry of relationship between bodies: how planets meet, support, challenge, intensify, and answer one another without flattening every contact into “good” or “bad.”

  • Conjunction, opposition, trine, square, and sextile
  • Planetary examples rather than abstract definitions alone
  • Applying and separating motion when the foundation is ready
✦

Foundation for synthesis

Chart Basics

How placements become a whole: glyph literacy, a useful reading order, and the patient work of joining planet, sign, house, and aspect without making any one symbol carry the entire story.

  • Reading the wheel and recognizing its symbols
  • Observation separated from interpretation
  • Synthesis instead of placement-by-placement fortune cookies
↻

Later study

Cycles & Transits

Timing, repetition, movement, and current conditions belong here, but only after the static grammar is strong enough to support them. This doorway is intentionally marked as later work.

  • Planetary cycles and meaningful recurrence
  • Transits as conditions, not guaranteed events
  • Timing without fatalism

The publishing rule

Learn it. Source it. Make the learning visible.

Separate tradition from my interpretation. Publish only when it can genuinely help. Revise without pretending the first version was carved into stone.

  • Name the tradition or school when systems disagree.
  • Cite sources and carry a visible last-reviewed date.
  • Avoid fatalistic claims, identity traps, and manufactured authority.
  • Treat every placement as part of a whole chart.
